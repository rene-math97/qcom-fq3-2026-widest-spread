The Setup

Four Things That Are True Going In

Every figure is sourced. My arithmetic is marked computed. This page is narrower than the others in this series, and section five explains exactly why.

01

The Street Models a Down Year, and Says Hold

Consensus has FY2026 revenue at $42.60B, down 3.80%, and FY2026 EPS at $10.81, down 10.18%.[2] A second source has EPS this year at −10.44% and next year at +1.65%.[4] So the base case on the tape is a decline followed by a flat year. Against that, 36 analysts produce an average target of $220.57, implying 35% upside, while rating the stock Hold. Those two things do not naturally sit together, and the tension is the story.

02

Gross Margin Has Eroded Four Quarters Running

The cleanest verifiable trend in the financials, and it points one way: 55.56% → 55.34% → 54.55% → 53.77% across the June 2025 through March 2026 quarters — a cumulative 179 basis points computed.[3] Operating margin fell further, from 26.65% to 21.79%, because operating expense did not fall with it. Whatever one believes about the long-term customer mix, the margin line is already moving, and it is moving before the most-discussed transition is complete.

03

Headline EPS Is Unreadable Without the Adjustment

Qualcomm's GAAP and non-GAAP figures have diverged violently. The March 2026 quarter reported GAAP diluted EPS of $6.88 on operating income of just $2,309M — a net income of $7,370M that plainly contains large non-operating items. The September 2025 quarter reported a GAAP net loss of $3,117M despite $2,918M of positive operating income.[3] Non-GAAP EPS for those quarters was $2.65 and $3.00. Any comparison against consensus must use the non-GAAP series, and this page does throughout.

04

The Operating Business Still Throws Off Cash

The bear case is about direction, not about quality. Qualcomm generates $12.50B of free cash flow, a 7.28% yield, with ROE of 36.08% and ROA of 12.69%, pays a $3.68 dividend yielding 2.16% at a 39.55% payout ratio, and trades at 17.8× trailing and 14.9× to 16.6× forward earnings.[1] That is a cheap, cash-generative business. The question the $100 target asks is whether the earnings base itself is about to step down.

A dispersion this wide on a $172B market cap with 36 analysts covering it is not a modelling disagreement about next quarter. It is a disagreement about the terminal shape of the business. Compare the other names reporting this week: Visa's published targets run $330 to $450, a 1.36× ratio. Qualcomm's is 3.14×. The consensus rating of Hold is what you get when you average two confident and opposite views, and it conveys almost no information.

Recent movement has been upward. Two upgrades appear in the recent record: DZ Bank from Hold to Buy with a $265 target, and Morgan Stanley from Underweight to Equal-Weight at $231.[4] Both moved toward the middle rather than to an extreme, which is consistent with a market that has stopped believing the worst case without yet believing the best one.

Gross margin has fallen in each of the last three sequential comparisons and is down 179 basis points over the four-quarter window computed. The operating margin move is larger — from 26.65% to 21.79% — because operating expense did not scale down with revenue in the March quarter. Note that the December quarter had the highest revenue and the second-highest operating margin of the four, so this is not simply seasonality; the margin erosion continues through a strong revenue quarter.

One reported item worth watching, flagged as thin. A summary of recent coverage indicates Qualcomm plans to raise chip prices in September.[4] If accurate, that is a direct management response to the margin trend above and would be a meaningful item on Wednesday's call. Single secondary source — I was unable to trace this to a Qualcomm release or a primary report, so it is presented as a thing to listen for, not as an established fact.

The Bar

Guidance, Consensus, and a Conflict

Company Guidance for FQ3 2026
Revenue = $9.2B to $10.0B
Non-GAAP EPS = $2.10 to $2.30

Consensus per Investing.com sits at $2.23 of EPS on $9.68B of revenue — both comfortably inside the guided bands, with EPS in the upper half.[5] A second source independently carries $2.23 for next-quarter EPS.[4] That is the number to measure Wednesday against.

A conflict I am disclosing rather than resolving. A third tracker publishes FQ3 consensus as $1.93 of EPS on $10.2B of revenue.[6] Both figures are inconsistent with the company's own guidance in opposite directions — the EPS sits below the guided floor of $2.10 while the revenue sits above the guided ceiling of $10.0B. That pattern strongly suggests a different accounting basis or a stale estimate vintage rather than a genuine view. I have used the $2.23 / $9.68B pair because two independent sources agree on it and it reconciles with guidance, but readers encountering $1.93 elsewhere should know it exists and why it is not used here.

Why there is no DCF here. The variable that determines Qualcomm's terminal value is customer concentration in the handset business, which is a binary contractual question rather than a smooth growth rate. Discounting a cash flow stream whose existence is the actual dispute would produce false precision. Two variables, both exposed, is the honest structure.

The margin trend continues

Med-High

Four consecutive quarters of gross margin erosion, with operating margin down nearly five points, is a trend rather than a blip. If FQ3 prints a fifth decline, the market will extrapolate — and the street's already-negative FY2026 estimates would start to look optimistic rather than conservative.

Counter: reported plans to raise prices in September would directly address it, if confirmed on the call.

The $100 target is right

Medium

Someone covering this stock believes it is worth 38% less than today. Reproducing that requires roughly a 20% earnings step-down combined with a trough multiple. Both are possible simultaneously if concentration risk resolves badly, and the model on this page shows exactly how those two assumptions compound.

Balance: someone else published $314, and two firms upgraded recently. The dispersion is genuine, not a stale outlier.

Upside: it is genuinely cheap

Low-Med
