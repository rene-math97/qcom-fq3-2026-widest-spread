The Setup

Four Things That Are True Going In

Company statements are quoted verbatim from the FQ2 2026 earnings call and the Form 10-Q. My arithmetic is marked computed. Where sources conflict, both appear.

01

The Largest Customer Loss Is Dated, Not Speculative

Asked directly about Apple on the April 29 call, CFO Akash Palkhiwala answered: "No change, Tim, to our assumption there. We have said 20% share of the phones that will launch in fall this year and no product relationship beyond that."[2] On the FY2027 revenue line he added that sell-side models around "a little over $2 billion" of QCT product revenue are "a reasonable place to model the business."[2] The 10-Q is blunter still: Apple "will increasingly use its own modem products, rather than our products, in its future devices, which will have a significant negative impact on our QCT revenues, results of operations and cash flows."[1] Most bear cases are forecasts. This one is guidance.

02

The Replacement Plan Is Real, Large, and Barely Started

At its June 24 Investor Day, Qualcomm raised the FY2029 non-handset revenue target to $40B, roughly double the prior target: automotive $10B, IoT more than $14B, data center more than $15B, with handsets falling to about one-third of QCT revenue and non-GAAP EPS above $18.[3] Against those targets: automotive is annualising near $5.3B and IoT near $6.9B computed — both need roughly to double in three years. Data centre is the stretch. It currently sits inside "nonreportable segments" at $141M of revenue and negative $121M of EBT for the quarter.[1]

03

The Near-Term Weakness Is Memory, and Management Dated the Bottom

This is the most actionable line in the release. Qualcomm attributes the China handset softness to memory prices, not end demand: "QCT handset shipments in China were significantly below end consumer demand due to memory-driven OEM build reductions and channel inventory drawdowns," and on the call, "Sell-through held up, and our chip business materially under-shipped consumer demand."[2][4] Management then committed to a turn: "We now estimate that QCT handset revenues from Chinese customers will reach a bottom in the third quarter and return to sequential growth in the following quarter."[4] That is a falsifiable claim about the quarter being reported.

04

Last Quarter's Headline EPS Was a Tax Event

The March quarter reported GAAP diluted EPS of $6.88 against non-GAAP of $2.65 — a gap I flagged in an earlier version of this page without being able to explain it. The cause is now clear: a $5.7B income tax benefit, worth $5.33 per share, from releasing a valuation allowance following new US Treasury and IRS guidance on the corporate alternative minimum tax. It is excluded from non-GAAP.[4] Every comparison on this page uses the non-GAAP series, and FQ3 guidance is GAAP $1.26–$1.46 against non-GAAP $2.10–$2.30 — a reminder that the two will diverge again.

Qualcomm's own 10-Q notes Apple buys "MDM (or thin modem) products, which do not include our integrated application processor technology, and which have lower revenue and margin contributions than our combined modem and application processor products."[1] Losing Apple removes revenue that was already dilutive to blended margin — which is why the earnings impact should be less than proportional to the revenue impact.

Qualcomm collects royalties on handsets whether or not its chips are inside them. Management: "Pending the renegotiation, royalty, we do not expect it to change… it should be in the same scale that it is at, and it is an independent business separate from the chip business."[2] QTL earned a 72% EBT margin last quarter. The catch is timing: Apple's licence expires in fiscal 2027, the same window in which the chip relationship ends, so both negotiations land at once.
One Unresolved Conflict, Flagged Rather Than Smoothed

Qualcomm guides to 20% of fall-2026 launches with nothing after. But documents stolen in a cyberattack on Apple manufacturing partner Tata Electronics, reported July 2, suggest the iPhone 18 Pro will use a Qualcomm modem in US models and Apple's own C2 internationally, reportedly for mmWave reasons.[5] Stolen documents If accurate, that implies materially more than 20% of units — or it reconciles if "20%" refers to models rather than volume. Separately, other reporting has the entire iPhone 18 line moving to the C2.[6]

The outlet reporting the leak states it has not seen the files directly. No Apple statement on any of this exists in 2026 — I looked and found nothing. Treat the company guidance as the base case and the leak as an unresolved upside tail, not as competing facts of equal weight.

What Replaces It

The $40 Billion Bet

Targets set at the June 24, 2026 Investor Day, against what the business actually earns today.[3] The gap between these two columns is the entire investment debate.

Automotive — FY2029 Target
$10B
Raised from the prior target. Annualising near $5.3B today, growing 38% and guided to ~50% next quarter. Design-win pipeline expanded to $65B. Management expects to exit FY2026 "above $6 billion" run-rate. This is the leg that is working.
IoT — FY2029 Target
>$14B
Split into industrial, networking and robotics at $8B, plus personal AI and compute at $6B. Annualising near $6.9B today and growing 9%. Needs to roughly double.
Data Center — FY2029 Target
>$15B
From $141M of combined nonreportable revenue today, at negative $121M of EBT. This is the single largest leap in the plan and the least proven.
FY2029 Non-GAAP EPS
>$18
Against street FY2026 EPS of $10.81. Handsets fall to roughly one-third of QCT revenue. Hitting $18 would make today's $162.88 price about 9× FY2029 earnings computed.
Data Centre — What Is Contracted vs. What Is Aspiration
  • Meta, announced June 24, 2026. A "strategic multi-generation agreement" under which the Dragonfly C1000 data centre CPU "will power Meta's next-generation server fleet," with a Mark Zuckerberg quote in Qualcomm's own release. No volumes or revenue disclosed, and the C1000 does not enter production until the second half of 2028.[8]
  • An unnamed hyperscaler, shipping December 2026. Amon: "a leading hyperscaler custom silicon engagement is on track for initial shipments later this calendar year," specified on the call as December.[2][4] The customer is not named in any source I could find, and it is a different, earlier programme than Meta's.
  • HUMAIN, Saudi Arabia. The first announced AI-infrastructure customer, for systems supporting 200MW of capacity.
  • The product roadmap. AI200 accelerators commercially available in 2026, AI250 in 2027, the C1000 CPU in production H2 2028.
  • The acquisitions behind it. Alphawave closed, contributing $2,274M of net assets with $2,215M of goodwill allocated specifically to the Data Center segment.[1] And in June, Modular — up to 19.2 million shares, reported at roughly $3.9B to $4B though Qualcomm has never stated a price — an explicit move at Nvidia's CUDA software moat via the Mojo language and MAX inference engine.[9][10]

A number I am not using. Third-party Investor Day write-ups put data centre revenue at roughly $0.3B in FY2026 and $5B in FY2027, with two hyperscalers contributing over $1B each. Those figures appear only in secondary renderings of the slides and could not be found in any Qualcomm-issued document. Only the ">$15B by FY2029" figure is company-sourced, so only it appears above.

Why This Dispersion Exists
Spread = $314 − $100 = $214
As a share of price = $214 ÷ $162.88 = 131%
Ratio = $314 ÷ $100 = 3.14×

Compare Visa, reporting the same week: published targets of $330 to $450, a ratio of 1.36×. Qualcomm's is 3.14×. The reason is now legible rather than mysterious. Both camps agree the handset business declines — Qualcomm told them so. They disagree entirely on whether $40B of non-handset revenue by FY2029, including a data centre business currently earning $141M at a loss, arrives. A $314 target is roughly the $18 FY2029 EPS target at a market multiple. A $100 target assumes the bridge does not get built.

Recent movement has been toward the middle. Two upgrades appear in the recent record: DZ Bank from Hold to Buy at $265, and Morgan Stanley from Underweight to Equal-Weight at $231.[12] Neither moved to an extreme, which is what you would expect from a market that has stopped believing the worst case without yet underwriting the best one.

The Rest of the Docket

Three Live Items

Arm Litigation
Oct 5
Qualcomm won at trial in December 2024, and on September 30, 2025 the court entered final judgment upholding the verdict and dismissing Arm's remaining claims. Arm appealed to the Third Circuit on October 1, 2025; no ruling yet. Qualcomm's own affirmative case against Arm goes to trial October 5, 2026. Arm withdrew its licence-termination threat in January 2025. Qualcomm records no accrual, judging losses "reasonably possible" but not probable.[1]
China Exposure
46%
Of FY2025 revenue by customer headquarters — $20,340M.[13] China Q2 2026 smartphone shipments fell 4.3% per IDC or 2% per Counterpoint, a fifth consecutive quarterly decline, while global shipments fell 11% to the lowest Q2 since 2013. Only Huawei (+24%) and Apple (+23%) grew — one uses in-house silicon, the other is leaving.
September Price Rise
Double digit
Bloomberg reported on July 24, from a letter to clients, that Qualcomm will raise prices by a double-digit percentage on products shipped after September 1, saying it can no longer absorb supplier costs. Qualcomm declined to comment and Reuters could not independently verify it.[14] Every other outlet traces to the same scoop.
SAMR / Autotalks
Open
China's regulator opened an antitrust probe in October 2025 into Qualcomm's ~$350M Autotalks acquisition for failing to file for clearance. The FQ2 2026 10-Q contains zero mentions of Autotalks or SAMR and its legal note lists only ParkerVision and Arm — implying Qualcomm does not consider it material. No 2026 resolution found.

On the price increase, and why it matters more than it looks. If confirmed on the call, a double-digit price rise effective September 1 is a direct management answer to the 179 basis points of gross margin erosion documented above — and it lands in FQ4, the quarter management has already said will return to sequential growth. It would also be a genuine test of pricing power in a market where global shipments just fell 11%. Listen for whether it is addressed at all.

What Would Make This Wrong

Risks in Both Directions

The bridge does not get built

Med-High

Data centre needs to go from $141M of combined nonreportable revenue at a loss to more than $15B in three years. The named customer engagements are real but back-loaded — Meta's C1000 does not enter production until H2 2028. If that leg slips, the $40B target goes with it and the handset decline has no offset.

Counter: automotive is genuinely working — 38% growth, guided to 50%, a $65B design-win pipeline, and an exit run-rate above $6B.

The margin trend continues

Med-High

Four straight quarters of gross margin erosion with operating margin down nearly five points. A fifth decline invites extrapolation, and the street's already-negative FY2026 estimates start looking optimistic rather than conservative.

Counter: the reported September price increase directly targets this, and losing Apple removes a below-average-margin product from the mix.

Both Apple negotiations land in fiscal 2027

Medium

The chip relationship ends after the fall 2026 launches and the patent licence expires in March 2027. Management says royalties should stay "in the same scale," but that is a negotiating position stated in advance, and Qualcomm's leverage is lower once it is no longer supplying silicon.

Structural defence: QTL earns royalties on handsets regardless of whose chips are inside, at a 72% EBT margin, and all OEM licences run to fiscal 2027–2031.

China is 46% of revenue in a shrinking market

Medium

Five consecutive quarterly declines in China shipments, global shipments at the lowest Q2 since 2013, and the only two share gainers are Huawei, which uses in-house silicon, and Apple, which is leaving. The 10-Q separately warns Chinese customers may develop their own chips under government self-sufficiency pressure.

Management's claim: the weakness is memory-driven build cuts and channel drawdown, not end demand — sell-through "held up" and shipments "materially under-shipped consumer demand."

Upside: it is genuinely cheap, and the plan is credible

Low-Med

Cuts both ways. A 7.28% free cash flow yield, 36% ROE, a 39.55% payout ratio, 14.9× to 16.6× forward earnings, 37% below the 52-week high, a fresh $20B buyback authorisation, and two recent upgrades. If FY2029 delivers the guided $18 of EPS, today's price is about 9× that number.

Evidence: five beats in six quarters with surprises consistently in the +2% to +4.5% band.
